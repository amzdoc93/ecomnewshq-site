Amazon's Silent FBA Warehouse Cleanup: Why Your Old Inventory Is Suddenly Selling

May 10, 2026 · 7 min read · By EcomNewsHQ Editorial

You open Seller Central, glance at sales by ASIN, and there it is: a SKU that hasn't moved a unit in eighteen months is suddenly racking up orders. A bug? A glitch in the new reports? Neither. Ed Rosenberg, along with dozens of sellers on LinkedIn and FBA forums, are reporting the same pattern across accounts of every size: Amazon is quietly surfacing dormant FBA inventory across the catalog, and it's about to break your Q2 forecast if you don't move fast.

This isn't a localized A/B test. The cleanup is system-wide, it's been escalating for ten days, and it has direct consequences for your reorders, your storage fees, and your PPC posture for the rest of the quarter. Here's what's actually happening and what to do about it.

What Is Actually Happening with Aged FBA Inventory

Sellers across multiple categories — supplements, home goods, beauty, pet, even apparel — are seeing the same signature:

  • SKUs with zero sales for 12-24 months are receiving orders
  • The orders are spread across multiple buyers, not single bulk pulls — this rules out a coordinated discount or vendor purchase
  • The affected ASINs are still active and in stock, but were buried in search rank
  • Sellers who did not adjust price, run PPC, or change anything else are seeing the lift — meaning Amazon, not the seller, is the variable that changed

The simplest explanation that fits: Amazon's search and recommendation engine has temporarily de-prioritized recency in favor of inventory clearance, surfacing aged ASINs to clear warehouse capacity.

Why Amazon Is Doing This Now

There are three plausible drivers, and they probably stack:

  1. Q1 close pressure on capacity metrics — Amazon reports utilization-related metrics quarterly. Empty warehouse space is reportable real estate Amazon would rather convert into Q2 inbound capacity for Prime Day buildup.
  2. Long-term storage fee revenue is plateauing — Amazon doesn't make sustainable revenue from aged inventory. They make it from velocity. Pushing aged SKUs through search clears the slot and re-creates fee opportunity.
  3. The 2024-2025 capacity restrictions — sellers were quietly pushed to send less. Some ASINs ended up over-stocked relative to actual demand. Amazon is now surfacing these to balance.

Whatever the precise driver, the operational reality is the same: aged inventory is moving in ways your historical sell-through models did not predict.

How This Breaks Your Inventory Forecast

Most replenishment tools — Forecastly, Restock Pro, Sellerboard's restock module, Inventory Planner — extrapolate from a trailing 30-90 day sales window. When a previously dormant ASIN starts moving 5-10 units a day, the forecast immediately suggests reordering. Two things go wrong if you trust that signal blindly:

  • You commit cash to a SKU that may not have a sustained demand profile — once the warehouse cleanup pass ends, search rank reverts and the lift evaporates
  • You lose Buy Box flexibility on your active SKUs — capital and capacity used on a one-time bump means less for the products that are actually trending

The right move is to flag every newly-moving aged SKU and treat its sales lift as suspect until it survives a full cycle.

The 15-Minute Account Audit

Before your next replenishment decision, run this check:

  1. Open Inventory Performance → Inventory Age report. Filter for SKUs with FBA age ≥ 180 days.
  2. Cross-reference against the Sales by ASIN report for the trailing 14 days. Flag any SKU that has age ≥ 180 days AND sales in the last 14 days where prior 90 days had zero or near-zero.
  3. For each flagged SKU, pull Search Term Impressions — if impressions are spiking on terms you don't actively bid on, Amazon's organic engine is doing the lift, not your PPC.
  4. Check Detail Page Sales and Traffic — if sessions are up but conversion is unchanged, the surfacing is real. If conversion is also up, you may have a genuine demand shift on top of the surfacing.
  5. Note which SKUs you would have reordered on this signal alone. Pause those reorders until you have at least 21 more days of data.

Four Steps to Protect Margin Over the Next 30 Days

  1. Right-price aged inventory while the surfacing window is open. If a previously stuck SKU now has buyers, this is the window to nudge price upward 5-10% on items that were under-priced for clearance. You're not gouging — you're capturing margin during a temporary demand event.
  2. Boost listing quality on the surfaced ASINs. Many aged ASINs have outdated images, weak A+ content, or stale keywords. Updating them now compounds with the search lift Amazon is already giving you.
  3. Initiate aged-inventory removal requests on the bottom 20%. The ASINs that did NOT benefit from the surfacing — still no sales after 14 days — are confirmed dead. Remove them before Q3 capacity tightens.
  4. Pause PPC on surfaced ASINs for 7 days. If organic is driving the bump, you don't want to pay for clicks you'd get for free. Re-enable PPC only after 7 days of organic data to see the real baseline.

If Your Dead SKU Is Suddenly a Hit

A small subset of sellers are seeing genuine breakthroughs — an ASIN that was dormant due to a seasonal miss or a slow listing now genuinely fitting a 2026 demand shift. The diagnostic question: would this product be selling at this rate if your competitor woke up tomorrow with the same inventory in their account? If yes (the demand is real), then:

  • Re-engage the supplier and lock in a refresh order at favorable terms
  • Update the listing to current standards before competitors notice and follow you in
  • Run a brand-defense PPC campaign on the search terms driving the lift
  • Add it back to your active replenishment forecast — but with conservative velocity assumptions for the first 60 days

When This Cleanup Cycle Likely Ends

Amazon doesn't announce these passes, but past behavior gives a reasonable estimate. Similar surfacing events in 2022 and 2024 lasted 3-6 weeks. Two signals to watch for the end:

  • Sudden drop in impressions on aged ASINs — when Amazon stops the surfacing, the impressions cliff is sharp, not gradual
  • Storage fee increase announcements — these often follow a capacity-clearing pass as the next lever Amazon pulls

Until then, treat every reorder decision on a previously-aged SKU with extra skepticism, and use the window to clear what isn't moving even now.

The sellers who handle this well in the next 30 days will exit Q2 with cleaner inventory, better margin on the products genuinely in demand, and capacity to scale into Prime Day. The ones who treat the surfacing as a real demand signal and reorder aggressively are about to overcommit cash on a phantom trend. Pick which side of that line you want to be on, and decide this week — not next month.
