Wise Business Review 2026: The International Payments Setup for Amazon Sellers

May 10, 2026 · 9 min read · By EcomNewsHQ Editorial

If you sell on Amazon US but don't live in the US, you have a problem most American sellers never think about: Amazon disburses your revenue in USD, your traditional bank doesn't have a US account, and converting through your local bank quietly eats 3-5% of every payout. Over $200K in annual revenue, that's $6,000-$10,000 burned on currency conversion that you never see itemized on any invoice.

Wise Business solves this problem better than any other tool on the market today, and at a lower setup cost than opening a US LLC and a US business bank. This review covers what Wise actually does for ecommerce sellers, the real fee structure, where it beats Mercury, and where it doesn't.

What Wise Business Actually Is

Wise (formerly TransferWise) operates a multi-currency banking layer for businesses. You open one account and receive local-bank-style account details in nine currencies, including USD, EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD, and SGD. Amazon, Shopify, Stripe, PayPal, and most US payment processors will pay into your USD account details exactly as they would pay a US business — no SWIFT transfer required, no intermediary bank fees, no foreign-transaction flag on the payer side.

You then hold the USD inside Wise, spend it on USD-denominated vendor invoices, pay USD-based VAs, or convert it to your local currency at the mid-market exchange rate plus a transparent fee (typically 0.4-0.6%). That single capability — receiving and holding USD without a US LLC — is why Wise has become the default rails for ~60% of non-US Amazon sellers we talk to.

Setup: What You Need and How Long It Takes

The Wise Business application is the closest thing to a smooth fintech onboarding in this industry. You'll need:

  • A registered business entity — sole proprietor, LLC, or corporation in your home country
  • Government-issued ID for the primary account holder
  • Proof of business address (utility bill, business registration, or lease)
  • Source of funds declaration (Amazon revenue is well-understood by their compliance team)
  • For some jurisdictions: business banking statement from your local bank

Approval timeline in our experience: 1-3 business days for European, UK, and Asian entities. North American applications typically clear same-day. Once approved, the USD account details activate immediately and you can paste them directly into Amazon Seller Central → Deposit Methods.

The Real Fee Structure (No Surprises)

Wise's fee model is the cleanest in the category. Three things cost money, the rest is free:

1. Account Opening

One-time fee, varies by registered region. Typical: $0-$50. North American businesses: $32. UK: £45. EU: €50. Many jurisdictions waive it during signup promotions.

2. Currency Conversion

0.4-0.6% over the actual mid-market rate, depending on currency pair and volume. For comparison: traditional banks charge 2-4% via spread (invisible) plus a fixed transfer fee. On a $10,000 Amazon disbursement converted to EUR, that's roughly $40-60 with Wise versus $200-400 with a traditional bank.

3. Local Wire Fees (Outbound)

Sending USD to a US vendor: $1-3 fixed. Sending GBP to a UK vendor: free. Sending EUR within SEPA: free. ACH payments inside the US (from your Wise USD account to a US supplier's account): free.

What's free:

  • Receiving payments from Amazon, Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments — into your USD account details
  • Holding USD (no monthly fee, no minimum balance)
  • Receiving payments in EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD, SGD, etc.
  • Debit card issuance (Wise Business Card)
  • Sending money between Wise accounts globally

Amazon Seller Central Integration

This is where Wise quietly wins. Amazon's Deposit Method screen accepts:

  • Routing number (9 digits, ACH format)
  • Account number (variable length)
  • Account holder name (must match your registered business entity exactly)

Wise provides all three in standard US format. Amazon treats the deposit as a domestic ACH transfer, which means:

  • No DD+7 currency hold (you get standard reserve cycle)
  • No "international deposit" flag that triggers compliance review
  • No FX conversion on Amazon's side — you receive USD into a USD account, and you choose when (and at what rate) to convert

For non-US sellers, that last point alone is worth the setup time: you control the conversion timing instead of Amazon converting on its schedule at its rate.

Where Wise Doesn't Replace a US LLC + Mercury

Wise is a payment infrastructure, not a banking license substitute. If your goal is:

  • Building US business credit — Wise won't report to D&B or Experian Business. A Mercury account tied to a US LLC will.
  • Accessing US credit cards or credit lines — most US fintech credit products (Brex, Amex Business, Ramp) require a US-registered entity and US bank statements. Wise statements work for some, not most.
  • Lower friction on tax reporting — if you eventually form a US LLC, having Mercury statements from day one simplifies bookkeeping, K-1 prep, and CPA review.
  • Faster ACH — Mercury's ACH typically settles in 1 business day; Wise's varies by jurisdiction and can take 2-3.

The honest answer most sellers we work with land on: Wise for the first 12-24 months until revenue justifies the LLC overhead, then add Mercury as the operating account for the US LLC and keep Wise for cross-currency settlement to the founder.

Who Should Open Wise Business This Week

You should open Wise within the next 7 days if:

  1. You sell or plan to sell on Amazon US (or any USD-denominated platform) from outside the United States
  2. You don't yet have a US LLC, or you have one but no operating account
  3. Your current Amazon deposits are converting through a local bank and you can't itemize the FX cost
  4. You're handling vendor payments in multiple currencies and losing money on each conversion
  5. You want to test US market entry without the $300-800 cost of forming an LLC first

You should wait on Wise if:

  • You already have a US LLC and a Mercury account performing well
  • You're a US tax resident with a US business — Mercury is structurally a better fit
  • You need to build US business credit history in the next 12 months

Bottom Line

For non-US Amazon sellers in 2026, Wise Business is the lowest-friction, lowest-fee way to receive USD payouts, hold them in multi-currency, and convert on your own schedule. It's not a US bank, it doesn't build credit, and it won't replace a real US LLC structure once your revenue justifies that step. But as the payment infrastructure for years 1 through 2 — and the cross-currency layer forever after — there's no better option in the category.

The $40-60 you'll save versus a traditional bank on a single $10K Amazon disbursement pays for the entire account opening fee. Everything after that is margin you keep.
